When a critical service depends on several technology providers, how do you keep the relationships visible across service, infrastructure and procurement records?

I have worked in IT infrastructure, operations and service delivery for more than two decades. In my experience, the challenge is often not a lack of supplier information; it is seeing how that information connects to the services people rely on. I built a small proof of concept to explore one part of that problem.

The Python tool uses fictional data to group services by declared provider. It flags providers appearing against multiple services and records whose dependency status is unconfirmed. In the sample, Northstar Cloud appears in four service records and Acme Identity in three. The output is a CSV review report and a JSON map.

These are review prompts, not supplier ratings. The program cannot identify dependencies missing from its input, determine the impact of a disruption or establish that recovery arrangements work. A real review would still need named owners, evidence and an agreed process for keeping records current.
